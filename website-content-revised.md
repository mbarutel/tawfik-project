### Executive Coaching

Executive coaching is a confidential hour where you don't have to have the answer yet. Most of what we cover is the stuff that's hard to raise anywhere else — the decision you keep circling, the conversation you've been putting off for a month, the read you're not quite sure you're getting right on someone in your team.

Over time the work tends to settle into a few areas. How you land in a room, and whether the leader people experience is the one you intend to be. How you decide when the information is never going to be complete. The patterns that got you here and the ones now quietly costing you — the things you do under pressure that you've never really stopped to examine. And, more often than people expect, the honest question of whether the way you're doing this job is a way you can keep doing it.

None of it is abstract. We work on live situations from your actual week, and you leave each session with something specific to try. Some of it works, some of it doesn't, and we talk about both.

It's tailored to you, but it isn't unstructured. We agree what success looks like at the start, check in halfway, and review it honestly at the end.

### What an Engagement Looks Like

1. A free 30-minute conversation. We talk about what's going on and whether coaching is the right tool. No pitch.
2. A goal-setting session. We agree what success looks like in plain language. Where your organisation is funding the work, we agree upfront what gets reported back — usually progress against goals, never the content of sessions.
3. Six coaching sessions, 60 to 75 minutes each, fortnightly or monthly across three to four months.
4. Work in between. Small, real, tested in your actual job — not homework for its own sake.
5. A mid-point check. Are we working on the right thing? If not, we change it.
6. A close-out review against the goals we set, and an honest conversation about what happens next.

### Coaching for Emerging & New Leaders

Making the step up stick.

**Button:** Book a free 30-minute chat

### Opening: The Honest Bit

The step from doing the work to leading the people doing the work is the biggest one in most careers, and it's the one organisations most often leave to chance. You get a title, a calendar full of meetings, and a quiet expectation that you'll work out the rest.

This coaching is about shortening that. We work on what the role actually asks for and nobody trains you in — renegotiating relationships with people who were your peers last month, handing over real work without hovering or disappearing, giving feedback that lands, setting expectations and then holding them when someone doesn't meet them.

Underneath all of it sits confidence. The real kind, not the performed kind. There's a difference between being settled and being very good at looking settled, and the second one is exhausting. Most new leaders are carrying more doubt than they let on, and naming it out loud with someone tends to take a surprising amount of weight off.

We'll also spend time on what kind of leader you're going to be. Not a values poster — a specific, honest answer to what people can count on from you, and whether your behaviour on a bad day matches it.

Available one-on-one, or as a group program for a cohort of leaders inside the same organisation.

### For Organisations: Why Early

It's cheaper to build a leader than to repair one.

Leadership problems in the first years rarely stay contained. They surface later as turnover in the team below, as grievances and complaints, as a culture that quietly drifts, and eventually as a performance process for someone who was, not long ago, one of your best people.

Coaching early costs a fraction of any of those. It's also the clearest signal you can send a high-potential person that you're serious about them, which matters, because those are exactly the people with options.

### What You Walk Away With

- A practical leadership toolkit: frameworks and conversation guides for the situations that come up weekly
- Language for delegation, feedback and expectation-setting that you can use straight away
- A clearer sense of your own strengths, and how to lead from them rather than borrowing someone else's style
- Habits that survive contact with a busy week
- For group programs: a peer network you keep, and a shared language across your leadership layer

Whether it's for you or for the leaders coming up behind you, start with a conversation.

### Team Workshops

Teams that work well together.

**Button:** Book a free 30-minute chat

### Opening: The Workshop Conversation

Most teams already know what's wrong. They just haven't said it in the same room, at the same time, with someone there to keep it safe.

A workshop is a structured version of that conversation. Depending on what your team needs, it might focus on trust, whether people can disagree, admit a mistake or raise a risk without first working out what it'll cost them. It might be about how the team makes decisions, and whether anything actually happens once the meeting ends. It might be taking values off the wall and turning them into behaviours people can hold each other to on a Tuesday when someone's dropped the ball. Often, it's simpler than any of that: building a shared language, so "urgent", "done" and "I'll take that on" mean the same thing to everyone in the room.

What it isn't is a day out with butcher's paper. Each session is designed around your team specifically, built on what surfaces in confidential pre-work rather than what leadership assumes is going on. The day is paced so the harder conversation has somewhere to go.

Half day, full day, or a series of shorter sessions. Works best with teams of six to fifteen.

### How a Workshop Is Built

This is where “designed around your team” gets proven rather than claimed.

1. Scoping conversation with you. What's happening, what's already been tried, and what a good outcome would actually look like.
2. Pre-work with the team. A short confidential survey so the design is built on what's really in the room rather than what leadership assumes is in the room.
3. Design, agreed with you. You see and agree on the summary of work.
4. The workshop itself. Facilitated, structured, and paced so the hard conversation has somewhere to go.
5. Facilitation and reflection. The session is facilitated, with space to reflect on what surfaced.
6. A follow-up session eight to twelve weeks later. Optional, strongly recommended, and the single biggest factor in whether anything actually changes.

### Formats

- Half day: one focused theme, best for a team that's largely working well
- Full day: the standard, and enough room for a genuine conversation plus decisions
- A series of shorter sessions: spread across a few months, good for teams that can't lose a whole day
- Offsite or planning day: where the team work sits alongside your strategy or planning agenda

> Works best with teams of six to fifteen. Larger groups are possible with a co-facilitator or a different design.

### When a Workshop Isn't the Answer

A workshop won't fix a team that's under-resourced, a structure that doesn't work, or a leader who isn't genuinely willing to hear what comes back. It also isn't the right tool for an active grievance or a serious interpersonal breakdown; those need a different process first.

If that's the situation, I'll tell you in the scoping call rather than take the booking. A workshop run over the top of an unaddressed problem does more damage than doing nothing.

If your team has a conversation that's overdue, let's talk about how to have it well.

I've been lucky. Early in my career I worked in high-stakes environments for leaders who could have just told me what to do and didn't. They stretched me, coached me, and kept handing the question back until I found the answer using what I already had. My whole trajectory comes from that. It took me a while to understand what leadership actually was. I thought it was a title. Then I realised it's a set of capabilities that either show up in everything you do, including the parts of your life nobody's watching, or they don't.

The thing I believe most: healthy organisations outperform smart ones over time. And health gets built through trust, confidence and people genuinely being happy and passionate about the work they do.

If you sat down with me, you'd hear me ask "what's beneath the surface here?" more than once, usually right after you've explained the problem at length. It's not a trick. The first version of a problem is rarely the real one.
